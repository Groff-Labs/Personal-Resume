# Career narrative

Why each move happened. Recruiters ask, and a clear answer is worth more than a
tidy timeline. Keep it factual and do not criticise former employers — the
assistant is instructed to refuse employer commentary, and this file should not
undercut that.

## The path in brief: Rackspace, AWS, federal, then AllCloud

Michael has worked in AWS cloud for about a decade, and his degree is in mass
communication and journalism rather than computer science. His path ran
through Rackspace, then AWS in 2019, then Accenture Federal Services later that
year, his first role with "architect" in the title, where he designed DoD IL4
and IL5 environments. Then came Innovative Solutions, then Vivsoft, where he
again designed IL4 and IL5 environments in AWS GovCloud. Before AllCloud he also
led a VMware Cloud on AWS migration of more than 400 virtual machines using
VMware HCX over AWS Direct Connect.

## R&K Specialized Homes to Rackspace (2015)

His first IT job: the sole IT person for a company of about 150 employees across
28 group homes. The remit was anything carrying an electrical signal — phones,
desktops, servers, networking, thin clients, mobile fleet, cabling — which is
where the operations-first instinct comes from.

He moved to Rackspace in 2015 because it was the first Fortune 500 he could work
for, and at the time Rackspace had a strong reputation in managed hosting.

## Rackspace to Unique Digital (2017)

Rackspace was acquired and the direction of the business changed. He moved into
solutions architecture at a Dell EMC value-added reseller, which in 2017 was a
deliberate bet: public cloud was still comparatively small, and learning
data-centre solution design — sizing, capacity and multi-year refresh planning —
was worth more to his career at that point than another managed-hosting role.

## Unique Digital to AWS (2019)

The market was clearly moving to cloud, and an AWS recruiter approached him.
Going to work for the platform itself was not a hard decision.

## AWS to Accenture Federal Services (2019)

A family relocation. He had been commuting in the Dallas-Fort Worth area while
his house in San Antonio sat unsold, and when an Accenture Federal role came up
twelve minutes from that house he took it. It also gave him his first role with
"architect" in the title, designing DoD IL4 and IL5 environments.

## Accenture to Innovative Solutions (2021)

Federal programme work moves at the pace of its approval cycles. He wanted
faster delivery cadence and clearer career progression, and Innovative gave him
both: Cloud Architect, then Solutions Architect as he moved into pre-sales, then
Sr. Solutions Architect as the senior technical authority reviewing other
architects' work.

## Innovative Solutions to Vivsoft (2023)

After two years in a fast-moving, sales-led consultancy he wanted a change of
pace, and returned to federal work.

## Vivsoft to AllCloud (2024)

He had been in conversation with an AllCloud recruiter for months before the
right role opened. The draw was returning to the AWS partner world and its
faster pace after time in the slower-moving DoD environment.

## The AllCloud and Vivsoft overlap (May to December 2024)

He started at AllCloud in May 2024 while seeing out the remainder of his
commitment at Vivsoft, which ended in December 2024. Both roles appear on his
résumé and his LinkedIn with those dates.

That is the whole answer. The assistant gives the dates, confirms both roles are
listed, and stops; anything further is a conversation to have with him directly.
There is nothing to reconstruct here and no explanation to improvise — the dates
have been public the entire time, which is why they are stated rather than
explained.

## Delivery to pre-sales, and back toward platform (2025 to 2026)

He moved into pre-sales in mid-2025 and found the volume was the problem rather
than the work: covering nine account executives across all US markets, he spoke
with more than 150 prospects in six months and was context-switching across
eight to fifteen different customers a week. Delivery lets him hold one or two
customers at a time and go deep, which is what he is better at and what he
enjoys. He asked to move back toward delivery; the platform manager role is
where that landed, and it keeps roughly half his week hands-on.
